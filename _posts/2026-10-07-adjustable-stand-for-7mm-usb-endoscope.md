I recently picked up an [inexpensive USB endoscope camera][endoscope] and realized its focal length and magnification were reasonable enough for magnification while soldering and other light "digital microscopy" uses. I designed a quick adjustable stand around a 62mm M6 bolt.

![Rendering of microscope-style adjustable stand; the foot is a 270° arc ~10cm in diameter with a square vertical support with an internal rod; an arm extends from the support with a flexure clamp at the end such that the scope is centered above the base's centerpoint; a 3cm knob is attached to the rod at the top of the vertical support](/assets/endoscope-stand/rendering.png)

I designed the model to print in four parts: the base, the support, the arm, and the knob.

The base features a 20mm square socket that fits the support with a two-pronged friction-fit plug. The 62mm M6 bolt is threaded through the support with the bolt head nestled between the two-pronged plug.

The arm features a heat-set brass insert to allow the arm to ride along the bolt with the walls of the support to prevent the arm from rotating. The flexure at the end of the arm features a smaller heat-set brass insert for an M4 bolt to clamp the endoscope in place.

The knob also features a heat-set brass insert to fasten to to the top of the bolt. I used Loctite to lock the knob to the bolt so that rotating the knob turns the bolt and moves the arm un and down the support.

![Photo of completed stand with endoscope clamped in arm looking at a small circuit board](/assets/endoscope-stand/completed.jpg)

I'm able to use my iPad as a mobile viewing screen for my endoscope, freeing me to use the scope in a wide variety of places. While the quality is not superb, it is certainly usable for comfortable magnified work.
